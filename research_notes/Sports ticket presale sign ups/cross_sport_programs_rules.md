# Cross-sport presale programs (cards, carriers, loyalty, platforms, venues) and the rules on presales and resale, as of October 4, 2026

Research notes. Every factual line has an inline source. Search-snippet-only sources are marked "(snippet)". Pages fetched and read in full are primary unless marked otherwise.

Research limits:
- The session's web-search budget ran out partway through. After that, only known URLs could be fetched.
- The Ticketmaster, SeatGeek and AXS help-center articles were read through each site's own public help-center API, so their text is first-hand. Each has an "edited" date.
- Loyalty programs, wireless carriers and several venues are thinly covered (see Gaps).

Terms used below:
- **Shared code:** the same code goes to everyone, so it is easy to leak.
- **BIN presale:** "enter the first 6 digits of your card." Many people's cards share the same digits, so this is close to a shared code. You still have to pay with an eligible card.
- **Account-linked:** access tied to one signed-in account, such as Citi or Verizon linking to Ticketmaster, or Live Nation All Access.
- **Draw/lottery:** a random selection with purchase slots (for example the FIFA/Visa draw).

## 1. Credit cards: which delivered real sports presales in 2025-26, how they work, and the cheapest qualifying card

### Takeaway
Real sports presales in 2025-26 came mainly from five sources:
- **American Express:** NFL Official Payments Partner from the 2026 season. Amex Presale Tickets cover the NFL Draft, Super Bowl LXI and 2026 international games, plus the Dolphins/Hard Rock Stadium, MetLife (Jets/Giants) and Mercedes-Benz Stadium (Falcons/Atlanta United).
- **Chase:** Madison Square Garden (Knicks/Rangers, including a Knicks NBA Finals cardmember presale in May 2026) and Chase Center (Warriors/Valkyries).
- **Capital One:** NCAA events and the Capital One Orange Bowl. The Orange Bowl presale ran Sept 21–24, 2026.
- **Mastercard:** Dodgers Opening Day 2026, plus MLB Priceless packages.
- **Visa:** the FIFA World Cup 2026 Visa Presale Draw (now over).

Citi's sports offers are thin (select college baseball and the Harlem Globetrotters). No sports presales were found for U.S. Bank, Wells Fargo or Discover. Most card presales are BIN-based. Citi has moved its presales to account-linking specifically to keep tickets away from "scalpers, brokers, and bots."

- **Cheapest qualifying cards, based on the eligibility rules above.** Annual fees were not verified in this session.

## 4. Ticketing platforms: how Ticketmaster, SeatGeek and AXS presales work; SafeTix and transfers; 2025-26 policy changes; official resale exchanges

### Takeaway
Ticketmaster now does the following:
- Shows an "All In Price" (total including fees).
- Enforces ticket limits *across* accounts linked by the same name, email, billing address or card, and may cancel orders and ban buyers.
- Increasingly requires **Persona identity checks** (selfie and sometimes ID; the account name must match your legal ID) at sign-in and for artist presale sign-ups. Failing the check blocks new purchases.

Presale types range from shared codes (team, venue, radio, social) to BIN card codes to account-linked access (Citi, Verizon Shine, Live Nation All Access). SafeTix rotating barcodes defeat screenshots, and a transfer issues a new barcode. Ticketmaster is the official ticketing partner of the NBA and MLS, and runs league and team exchanges plus a Face Value Exchange and a CashorTrade (face-value) option. SeatGeek bans speculative listings and relies on access codes distributed by teams, venues and card partners. AXS presales are code-based.

### Cited Findings

**Ticketmaster: limits, accounts and identity (2025-26)**
- **Ticket limits across accounts.** "Multiple Ticketmaster accounts may not be used to circumvent or exceed published ticket limits. This includes accounts or orders associated with the same name, email address, billing address, credit card number or other information. We reserve the right to cancel your orders and tickets, and to prevent you, without notice, from making future purchases." — [Ticketmaster Help: Why is there a ticket limit?, edited Jan 14, 2026](https://help.ticketmaster.com/hc/en-us/articles/9781245025937-Why-is-there-a-ticket-limit).
- **Persona security checks.**
  - Fans may need to complete a Persona check: a live selfie and sometimes a government photo ID.
  - "The name on your Ticketmaster Account must match your legal name as it appears on your ID."
  - Checks happen at sign-in and at artist presale sign-up. A check at sign-in requires selfie plus ID.
  - "If the security check isn't completed or is unsuccessful, you won't be able to purchase new tickets from your account."
  - "The security check must be completed by the account holder signing up for the presale."
  - Persona keeps biometric data up to 60 days after a successful check and up to 3 years after a failed one.
  
  — [Ticketmaster Help, edited Aug 25, 2026](https://help.ticketmaster.com/hc/en-us/articles/37371579224209-Why-is-my-Ticketmaster-Account-requiring-a-Security-Check-through-Persona).
- **Accounts can't be merged.** "Ticketmaster Accounts can't be combined." — [Ticketmaster Help, edited Sep 19, 2025](https://help.ticketmaster.com/hc/en-us/articles/26797867792401-Can-I-merge-or-combine-my-Ticketmaster-Accounts).
- **Team and venue accounts use the same login.** Tickets live at am.ticketmaster.com, and the same email and password work across teams. NFL, NBA, MLB and NHL transfers often sit on the team site. Season tickets appear for resale "if your team has authorized resale." — [Ticketmaster Help: sports team or venue account, edited Aug 12, 2026](https://help.ticketmaster.com/hc/en-us/articles/9760688108945-How-do-I-use-my-sports-team-or-venue-account).
- **Current policy versions.** The Purchase Policy, Reseller Policy and Terms of Use pages at legal.ticketmaster.com show "Effective Date: August 12, 2025." ## 6. Rules on presales and resale: BOTS Act, executive order, FTC Fees Rule, FTC/DOJ cases, TICKET Act, state laws, platform terms, and a do/don't list

### Takeaway
As of Oct 4, 2026:
- **Federal enforcement is active.** The FTC sued Live Nation-Ticketmaster (Sept 2025) and, in late Sept 2026, a judge let its BOTS Act claims proceed against the platform. The FTC also sued broker Key Investment Group (Aug 2025; the key question there is whether multi-account buying counts as a BOTS Act violation) and settled with Elite Events/smartscalpers.com (July 2026). In April 2026 it sued StubHub under the all-in Fees Rule, in force since May 12, 2025.
- **Antitrust case.** DOJ settled with Live Nation mid-trial (Mar 9, 2026). The states won a jury verdict (Apr 15, 2026); remedies and appeals are pending.
- **Federal legislation.** The TICKET Act passed the House in April 2025 but had not been enacted as of the latest source (Apr 2026).
- **States.** States are adding speculative-listing bans, anti-bot and presale-code-circumvention bans, and in a few places resale price caps:
  - Maine: 10% cap.
  - Vermont: 110% cap for small, independent and collegiate venues.
  - DC: 10% cap reported passed.
  - North Carolina: rules effective Oct 1, 2026.
- **Arizona and North Carolina** expressly outlaw using multiple accounts or bypassing presale codes or limits.

### Cited Findings

**BOTS Act and executive order**
- **What the BOTS Act prohibits.** Circumventing a ticket issuer's "security measure, access control system, or other technological control or measure" to buy more tickets than permitted, and selling tickets obtained that way. It applies to venues with capacity over 200. The FTC and state AGs enforce it. The first FTC cases came in 2021 (Just in Time Tickets, Concert Specials, Cartisim). — [CRS R48179, updated Apr 22, 2026](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-04-22_R48179_431e1c13f57808c089533dcde71e4bd6eebfd3b7.html).
- **Executive Order 14254, "Combating Unfair Practices in the Live Entertainment Market" (Mar 31, 2025; Federal Register Apr 3, 2025).** It directs the FTC to enforce the BOTS Act rigorously and to ensure price transparency. It directs Treasury and DOJ to make sure scalpers comply with tax law, and requires a progress report within 180 days. — [Federal Register PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-04-03/pdf/2025-05906.pdf); [CRS](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48179).

**FTC enforcement cases**
- **FTC v. Key Investment Group (Aug 2025, D. Md.).**
  - Alleged use of thousands of Ticketmaster accounts, masked IPs, SIM banks and virtual cards.
  - Over 2,200 Eras Tour tickets in 2023 and more than $1.2M in resale revenue.
  - KIG sued first (July 2025), arguing the FTC treats "multi-account ticket purchasing as if it were the same as running automated ticket bots."
  - Both sides moved to dismiss in Nov 2025 before Judge George L. Russell III.
  
  — [TicketNews, Nov 2025](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/11/ticket-broker-ftc-trade-motions-to-dismiss-in-bots-act-legal-battle/).
- **Elite Events (smartscalpers.com) settlement, July 28, 2026.**
  - Overseas "pullers" used fabricated emails, virtual credit cards and proxies to get around purchase limits on Ticketmaster and AXS across about 6,000 events.
  - Over 100,000 tickets resold on StubHub, Vivid Seats and SeatGeek.
  - $10.7M judgment, with $300,000 payable because of inability to pay.
  
  — [Law Commentary](https://www.lawcommentary.com/articles/ticket-broker-settles-with-ftc-over-bots-act-violations-tied-to-tate-mcrae-megan-moroney-tickets) (secondary source; FTC release not fetched).
- **FTC + 7 states v. Live Nation/Ticketmaster (filed Sept 18, 2025, C.D. Cal.).**
  - States joining: CO, FL, IL, NE, TN, UT, VA.
  - Allegations: bait-and-switch pricing; deceptive claims about ticket limits ("brokers routinely and substantially exceeded those limits"); and profiting from resale of broker-harvested tickets.
  - A senior executive is quoted saying Ticketmaster "turn[s] a blind eye as a matter of policy."
  
  — [FTC press release, Sept 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-sues-live-nation-ticketmaster-engaging-illegal-ticket-resale-tactics-deceiving-artists-consumers) (snippet); [Live Nation 10-Q, Q2 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001335258/000133525826000035/lyv-20260630.htm).
  - **Motion to dismiss.** Live Nation moved to dismiss in Jan 2026 — [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001335258/000133525826000035/lyv-20260630.htm). Judge Maame Ewusi-Mensah Frimpong denied dismissal of the BOTS Act claims: "The BOTS Act does not preclude platforms from facing liability." The case is now in discovery with no trial date. — [CelebrityAccess, Sept 30, 2026](https://celebrityaccess.com/2026/09/30/ticketmaster-must-face-ftc-ticket-resale-lawsuit-as-judge-says-bots-act-applies/).
- **FTC v. StubHub (SDNY, filed Apr 9, 2026).** Alleges violations of FTC Act §5 and the Fees Rule for failing to show the total price "every place price is displayed." StubHub had publicly supported the rule. — [FTC complaint PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/StubHub-Complaint.pdf).

**FTC Fees Rule**
- **Effective May 12, 2025 (16 C.F.R. Part 464).** The total price including all mandatory fees must be disclosed up front for live-event tickets. Drip pricing and partition pricing are prohibited. The rule covers primary sellers, resellers and promoters. — [FTC Fees Rule FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/rule-unfair-or-deceptive-fees-frequently-asked-questions); [StubHub complaint](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/StubHub-Complaint.pdf).
- **Date discrepancy.** The CRS summary as fetched gives "December 17, 2024." That appears to be the date the rule was finalized or announced, not when it took effect — [CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-04-22_R48179_431e1c13f57808c089533dcde71e4bd6eebfd3b7.html).

**DOJ/states v. Live Nation (antitrust)**
- **DOJ settlement (Mar 9, 2026).**
  - 15% cap on certain service fees.
  - Ticketmaster must offer venues exclusive and non-exclusive deals, and venues may use other primary ticketers for part of their tickets.
  - Divests 13 exclusive amphitheater booking agreements; promoters can sell up to 50% of amphitheater tickets as they choose.
  - Consent decree extended 8 years.
  - $280M fund for state damages claims.
  - No admission of wrongdoing.
  - Nothing about consumer resale or transfer.
  
  — [Live Nation statement](https://news.livenationentertainment.com/statements/live-nation-entertainment-reaches-settlement-with-u-s-department-of-justice).
- **Status after the verdict, per Live Nation's Q2 2026 10-Q.**
  - Proposed Final Judgment filed June 12, 2026; still subject to Tunney Act court approval.
  - Jury verdict for the litigating states on Apr 15, 2026.
  - Post-trial motions set for argument July 31, 2026; a remedies phase is to follow. The states sought remedies including divestiture of Ticketmaster.
  - Live Nation plans to appeal to the 2nd Circuit and recognized $450M in losses.
  
  — [Live Nation 10-Q, Q2 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001335258/000133525826000035/lyv-20260630.htm).
- **The verdict.** The jury found monopolization (major-venue ticketing, large amphitheaters) and illegal tying, with a $1.72-per-ticket overcharge. Remedies go to a separate bench trial. — [NY AG, Apr 15, 2026](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2026/attorney-general-james-and-coalition-states-win-trial-against-live-nation-and); [NPR, Apr 15, 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/04/15/nx-s1-5786715/live-nation-ticketmaster-antitrust-verdict-monopoly) (snippet).

**Federal bills**
- **TICKET Act** (all-in pricing, speculative-ticket ban, refunds, an FTC report on BOTS Act enforcement).
  - H.R. 1402 passed the House Apr 29, 2025 (409–15) and was placed on the Senate calendar.
  - S. 281 was reported by Senate Commerce.
  - Not enacted as of CRS (Apr 22, 2026).

- **MAIN Event Ticketing Act (H.R. 2713 / S. 196).**
  - Would expand the BOTS Act: issuers would have to maintain access controls and purchase limits and report incidents to the FTC within 30 days.
  - Penalties: at least $10,000/day plus $1,000 per violation.
  - Targets "circumvention… not merely violating purchasing terms."
  - Cleared House Energy & Commerce 36–0.
  
  — [TicketNews (URL dated Sept 2026)](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/main-event-ticketing-act-house-committee-bots-act/). **Date conflict:** the fetched text gave the committee vote as Sept 16, 2025, while the URL suggests Sept 2026.

### Inferences: do/don't list for using presales legitimately (especially if you resell some tickets)

**DO**
- Use **one account per platform, in your legal name**, with current phone and email. Ticketmaster's Persona checks require the account name to match your ID, and failing one blocks new purchases. ([TM Persona](https://help.ticketmaster.com/hc/en-us/articles/37371579224209-Why-is-my-Ticketmaster-Account-requiring-a-Security-Check-through-Persona))
- **Stay within the posted ticket limit across everything tied to you:** name, email, billing address and card. Ticketmaster counts these together and can cancel orders and ban future purchases. ([TM ticket limit](https://help.ticketmaster.com/hc/en-us/articles/9781245025937-Why-is-there-a-ticket-limit))
- For card presales, **pay with the eligible card in your own name.** Name and billing address must match (Capital One), and BIN presales require checkout with that card. Link Citi to Ticketmaster and relink every year. Connect Verizon in Ticketmaster Account Settings if you use Verizon Shine.
- **If you must resell, use sanctioned channels.** Options include Ticketmaster Face Value Exchange or CashorTrade, or the team's authorized exchange. Expect tax reporting.
- **Check where the event is.** Resale caps apply in Maine (10%), in Vermont (110%, only for certain venues including collegiate and amateur sports, and not for individuals reselling personal-use tickets), and in DC (10%, reported passed). New York's proposed caps excluded sports.
- **Only list tickets you actually hold.** Speculative listings are banned in MD, NY, ME, VT and NC (from Oct 1, 2026), and SeatGeek forbids them.

**DON'T**
- **Don't use multiple or alternate accounts, emails, phone numbers, cards or addresses to get more tickets than the limit.** It breaches Ticketmaster terms. It is expressly illegal in Arizona (HB 2040). The FTC is treating it as a BOTS Act issue: in the KIG case (contested) and in the Ticketmaster case, where the BOTS Act claims survived dismissal in Sept 2026.
- **Don't use bots, auto-buy or refresh tools, or anything that bypasses queues, waiting rooms or presale codes.** This falls under the BOTS Act and state laws in AZ, NC and ME. The pending MAIN Event Act would raise penalties.
- **Don't sell screenshots or barcodes.** SafeTix barcodes rotate every 15 seconds, and NFL games don't accept screenshots or printouts. Transfer properly; the recipient gets a new barcode.
- **Don't assume a presale code makes you eligible.** Codes don't guarantee tickets, and identity or card checks still apply. Artist presales require the account holder personally to complete any security check.
